The patient presents with a 6-week history of progressive shortness of breath, chest discomfort, and intermittent palpitations. He reports increasing fatigue over the past month and difficulty sleeping due to nocturnal dyspnea.

--------------------------
Mr. O'Connor is a 40-year-old software engineer who first noticed symptoms approximately 6 weeks ago. He describes the onset as gradual, initially occurring only with moderate exertion such as climbing stairs or walking uphill. Over the past 3 weeks, the symptoms have progressed significantly, now occurring with minimal activity and even at rest.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN
-------------------
Assessment:
1. Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF): The patient presents with classic signs and symptoms of heart failure, including dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, lower extremity edema, and physical examination findings of S3 gallop, displaced PMI, and bibasilar rales. Echocardiogram confirms severely reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (35%) with left ventricular dilation. BNP is significantly elevated at 485 pg/mL, consistent with heart failure. The etiology is likely non-ischemic cardiomyopathy, possibly related to his history of hypertension and former smoking, though further evaluation is needed.

2. Hypertension: Blood pressure elevated today at 142/88, though patient reports feeling anxious. Currently on lisinopril. Will need to optimize blood pressure control as part of heart failure management.

3. Hyperlipidemia: LDL remains elevated at 115 mg/dL despite statin therapy. Will need to intensify lipid-lowering therapy.
